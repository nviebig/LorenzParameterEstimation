Statistics-based ("climate") optimization in Lorenz-63: implementation-grounded draft

Overview
- Goal: Fit parameters to match long-run statistics of the Lorenz-63 invariant measure rather than time-resolved trajectories.
- Rationale: Trajectory matching breaks down beyond the Lyapunov time τ_λ ≈ 1.1, while ergodic statistics remain stable and differentiable.
- Implementation basis: `src/training_climate.jl` (module TrainingClimate) and exported API in `src/LorenzParameterEstimation.jl`.

Core API and configuration
- Entry points: `train_statistics`, `evaluate_statistics`, `ClimateConfig`, and PDF helpers (`soft_hist_3d`, `soft_hist_3d_local`, `hard_hist_3d`).
- `ClimateConfig`: controls integrator `dt`, total `steps`, initial conditions batch, samples per epoch `k`, RNG, loss mode, PDF grid/bandwidth via `PdfConfig`, and bandwidth schedule via `PdfSchedule`.
- `PdfConfig`: 1D centers shared across x,y,z, Gaussian bandwidth `h`, loss mode (`:kl`, `:cross_entropy`, `:wasserstein`), and Sinkhorn parameters for Wasserstein.
- `PdfSchedule`: anneals bandwidth from smooth to sharp over epochs: `h(epoch) = h_end + (h_start - h_end) * exp(-epoch/τ)`; defaults aim for early smoothing and later sharpening.

Statistics computed
- Moments: mean and standard deviation per coordinate via time subsampling (`evaluate_statistics`, `_stats_over_batch_subsample`).
- PDFs: 3D density over (x,y,z) using soft Gaussian histograms (`soft_hist_3d_local`) or hard histograms (`hard_hist_3d`).
- Subsampling: stratified/jittered indices (`stratified_indices`, `jittered_grid_burnin`) avoid transients and ensure broad attractor coverage without full backprop through long horizons.

Loss functions (Enzyme-friendly)
- Moment loss: central dispatcher `_stat_loss(a,b,mode)` with `:sse`, `:mse`, `:rmse`, or `:mae` applied to flattened [mean,std] vectors.
- PDF loss: `_loss_pdf_core_3d` builds soft 3D histogram for sampled points and compares to target via:
	- KL divergence: `_kl_divergence_vec`
	- Cross-entropy: `_cross_entropy_vec`
	- ε-regularized Sinkhorn Wasserstein: `_sinkhorn_wasserstein_nd` (small grids only; cost matrix is n×n with n=m^3).
- Differentiation: reverse-mode Enzyme wraps loss entries (`_loss_entry_stats`, `_loss_entry_pdf3d`) so gradients w.r.t. parameters (σ,ρ,β and shifts x_s,y_s,z_s,θ) are computed efficiently.

Training loop details (`train_statistics`)
- Per epoch:
	- Sample time indices I (burn-in + jitter) across a trajectory of `steps` with `dt`.
	- For each batch of initial conditions (columns of `U_epoch`), integrate forward once and compute selected statistics.
	- Compute loss (moments or PDF) against target; run Enzyme reverse-mode to obtain gradients.
	- Accumulate and average gradients across batch windows; apply update via Optimisers.jl (e.g., Adam).
	- Clip gradients (`_clip_grads`) to a configurable norm; mask updates via `DEFAULT_MASK` when training only a subset of parameters.
	- Record per-epoch statistics over the full trajectory; optionally collect detailed timing per section.
- Early stopping: monitors absolute/relative improvement with patience counter.
- Bandwidth annealing (PDF mode): smoothly reduces `h` across epochs to transition from coarse to fine PDFs, improving gradient quality early and accuracy later.

Why stable beyond τ_λ
- Statistics depend on ergodic averages and distributional features; they do not require backprop through the entire time horizon.
- Window reinitialization and subsampling avoid exploding adjoints while still sampling the attractor.
- Smooth histogram kernels (soft PDFs) make the objective differentiable and numerically well-conditioned.

Practical settings (Lorenz-63)
- Integrator: in-place RK4 stepper (`rk4_step!`) with `lorenz_rhs!`, `dt ≈ 0.01`, `steps ≈ 4000` per epoch trajectory.
- Sampling: `samples_per_epoch ≈ 256`, burn-in ≈ 30% of steps, jitter ≈ 0.35; use `evaluate_statistics` for full-trajectory stats when reporting.
- PDFs: centers length m (e.g., 16–64), bandwidth schedule from `h_start_mult ≈ 3.0` to `h_end_mult ≈ 0.7` times grid spacing Δ; loss mode `:kl` or `:cross_entropy` for larger grids, `:wasserstein` for small m.
- Optimizer: `Optimisers.Adam(1e-3)` with gradient clip norm ≈ 1.0; mask non-dynamic shifts unless explicitly desired (`DEFAULT_MASK`).

Targets and evaluation
- Moments target: NamedTuple, e.g. `(mean=[mx,my,mz], std=[sx,sy,sz])` gathered from a long reference run; shapes checked and flattened consistently.
- PDF target: NamedTuple with `centers` and normalized `p3d`; training warns if centers differ from config, using the config grid for consistency.
- Final statistics: `train_statistics` returns final params, loss history, per-epoch stats, parameter history, and optional timing report; PDFs evaluated with hardened kernel at end (`h_end`).

Observed behavior
- Convergence to parameter sets that reproduce reference mean/std and PDFs even when trajectory alignment is impossible beyond τ_λ.
- Stable gradients across epochs; occasional clipping near sharp PDF phases. Failures mostly due to too few bins, overly sharp bandwidth early, or excessive learning rates.

Implications for differentiable climate models
- Statistics-based objectives align with climate evaluation practice (climatologies, distributions, autocorrelations) and avoid gradient pathologies of long-horizon trajectory loss.
- The approach scales: subsampling, batch ICs, and smooth PDF objectives provide a practical template for higher-dimensional chaotic models and hybrid ESM components.
