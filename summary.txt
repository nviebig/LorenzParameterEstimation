\section{Optimization approaches in chaotic systems}
The Lorenz-63 system provides a compact testbed for studying how gradient-based optimization behaves in chaotic regimes. Because Lorenz-63 exhibits exponential sensitivity to initial conditions, any attempt to calibrate parameters from observational data is connected to the question of what loss function to choose to yield meaningful gradients in a chaotic system?

In practice, two different regimes emerge, following the same separation that exists in the atmosphere between short term weather predictability and long-term climate statistics: 
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Trajectory-based optimization ("weather")}, which attempts to match individual trajectories over short horizons.
    \item \textbf{Statistics-based optimization (“climate”)}, which matches long-term distributional properties
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Trajectory-based ("weather") optimization}

The Trajectory based optimization attempts to calibrate the model parameter by matching a reference trajectory to a target trajectory by minimizing a loss function. This involves simulating a system from a given initial condition and adjusting the model parameters to minimize the differences the models and the targets trajectory.
A key challenge in a chaotic system is the rapid divergence of trajectories due to sensitivity dependence on initial conditions. Even with nearly identical parameters and starting points, two trajectories will typically separate exponentially over time. As A result, direct trajectories matching becomes unstable or uninformative

\subsection{Statistics-based (“climate”) optimization}

Statistics-based optimization targets long-term statistical properties rather than individual trajectories.

\subsubsection{Invariant measures and ergodic averages}

Invariant measures and ergodic averages characterize the long-term behavior of chaotic systems.

\subsubsection{PDF, histogram, and moment matching}

Matching probability density functions (PDFs), histograms, and moments enables optimization of statistical properties.

\subsubsection{Stable gradients in chaotic regimes}

Statistical approaches can yield stable gradients even in highly chaotic regimes.
